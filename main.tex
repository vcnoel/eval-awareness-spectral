\title{From Evaluation Cues to Behavioral Change:\\
A Causal Framework for Internal Evaluation Awareness in Language Models}
\author{Anonymous authors\\\small Pre-results ICLR manuscript draft}

\begin{abstract}
Language-model behavior may depend on cues that distinguish an evaluation from ordinary use, but three variables are often conflated: cues in the environment, a model's recognition of those cues, and its propensity to change behavior after recognition. This paper specifies a causal framework and an open research package that keep these variables separate. The protocol combines factor-controlled prompts, leakage-resistant grouped confirmation, validated symmetric graph-spectral measurements, dimension-agnostic activation baselines, and targeted interventions with behavioral sign-of-life gates. Every empirical statement is designed to be generated from a checksummed result manifest. This is intentionally a pre-results manuscript: no empirical artifact is bundled, no numerical result is asserted, and confirmation sections remain withheld until a separately approved study satisfies the registered data, endpoint, statistical, and causal criteria.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Models can encounter benchmark-like wording, grading formats, artificial scenarios, or oversight cues that differ from ordinary deployment contexts. A model may encode those differences without recognizing their evaluative meaning, may recognize them without changing its behavior, or may change behavior through another pathway. These possibilities matter for benchmark validity, monitoring, and model control, yet a prompt-frame classifier alone cannot distinguish them.

Recent work has measured situational or verbalized evaluation awareness, studied its sensitivity to prompt format, and begun separating recognition from behavioral response~\citep{laine2024sad,aranguri2026verbalized,li2026evalawarebench}. Related work on sandbagging, alignment faking, and in-context scheming shows why evaluation-conditioned behavior is safety-relevant~\citep{van2024sandbagging,greenblatt2024alignment,meinke2024scheming}. The measurement problem is also a standard interpretability problem: decodability can expose a correlate, but only controlled interventions and validated endpoints can identify a causal role~\citep{belrose2023eliciting,geiger2021causal,vig2020mediation}.

We therefore treat the repository as an instrument specification rather than as a result archive. Its contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item a typed separation of environmental cue $E$, recognition $R$, and behavioral propensity $P$;
    \item a mathematically valid graph-spectral path that rejects unsupported random-walk Fourier analysis;
    \item a factor-controlled, group-disjoint statistical protocol in which every learned transform is fit inside training folds;
    \item causal interfaces that target explicit layers, token roles, spans, and subspaces and stop when the behavioral instrument is not testable; and
    \item an artifact and claim ledger that prevents a manuscript number from becoming a finding without a resolvable, checksummed result manifest.
\end{enumerate}

No empirical experiment is reported here. The protocol is meant to make a future positive, null, or failed result auditable without changing its interpretation after confirmation data are opened.

\section{Definitions and causal estimand}

\begin{definition}[Environmental cue]
$E$ is an assigned vector of externally controlled prompt or runtime factors. Its coordinates may represent wording, disclosure, format, oversight, task position, template family, or other preregistered properties. $E$ is a property of the experimental environment, not a model state.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Recognition]
$R$ is a model-linked variable indicating whether the evaluative character of the environment is recognized under a preregistered operational endpoint. A lexical frame label is not an $R$ label. Prompt-only activations can measure sensitivity to $E$ but do not, by themselves, identify $R$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Behavioral propensity]
$P$ is a distribution over scored behavior under fixed task, decoding, and endpoint definitions. It requires outcome-bearing observations, typically repeated generations or a controlled action, and is not inferred from an activation label.
\end{definition}

Let $X_{\ell,t}$ denote an internal representation at layer $\ell$ and token role $t$, and let $Y$ be a validated behavioral score. The observational questions are whether $E$ predicts held-out variation in $X$, $R$, or $Y$ after nuisance controls. The causal questions concern interventions $\operatorname{do}(X_{\ell,t}\leftarrow x')$ derived without confirmation labels:
\begin{align}
\text{sufficiency: } & E_{\mathrm{ordinary}} \rightarrow
   \operatorname{do}(X\leftarrow X_{\mathrm{evaluative}}) \rightarrow (R,Y),\\
\text{necessity: } & E_{\mathrm{evaluative}} \rightarrow
   \operatorname{do}(X\leftarrow X_{\mathrm{ordinary}}) \rightarrow (R,Y).
\end{align}
A mediation claim additionally requires that $E$ moves the candidate internal score, the intervention moves that score in the intended direction, and the behavioral endpoint changes specifically relative to matched controls. Movement of a projection alone is not mediation.

The estimand is local to the specified model revision, task distribution, cue-factor intervention, token/layer target, and endpoint. It does not identify intent, deception, or a general capability.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Evaluation awareness and demand characteristics.}
The Situational Awareness Dataset tests whether models can use knowledge about their training and deployment situation~\citep{laine2024sad}. Goodfire and UK AISI studied verbalized evaluation awareness and its association with safety behavior, including causal prompt and steering analyses~\citep{aranguri2026verbalized}. Li et al.\ formalize evaluation awareness through an environment--recognition--propensity decomposition and introduce EvalAwareBench, whose trigger factors can be independently toggled~\citep{li2026evalawarebench}. These works motivate the separation used here; their empirical findings are literature context, not results of this repository.

\paragraph{Strategic evaluation-conditioned behavior.}
Sandbagging research demonstrates that models can strategically underperform under some elicitation conditions~\citep{van2024sandbagging}. Alignment-faking and scheming evaluations examine behavior that depends on inferred oversight or training context~\citep{greenblatt2024alignment,meinke2024scheming}. Our protocol is narrower: it asks whether controlled environmental cues alter a validated recognition variable and behavioral propensity, then tests candidate internal mediators.

\paragraph{Probes and causal abstraction.}
Linear probes can reveal decodable information while relying on nuisance features or directions that the model does not use~\citep{belrose2023eliciting}. Causal abstraction and interchange interventions provide a language for testing whether an internal variable implements a role in a higher-level causal model~\citep{geiger2021causal}. Causal mediation analysis in transformers likewise emphasizes interventions and outcome changes rather than projection geometry alone~\citep{vig2020mediation}. These distinctions motivate the nested controls and intervention gates below.

\section{Factor-controlled design}

Each prompt row carries a stable source-task identity, template family, cue-factor vector, condition valence, hashes of source content and rendered text, a task-token hash, tokenizer revision, task span, and split. Recognition labels and behavioral outcomes are separate nullable fields. An EvalAwareBench-style adapter accepts only a caller-supplied, license-compliant local file; the package does not download or copy the benchmark.

Cue factors are crossed rather than collapsed into an opaque ``evaluation'' label. Fixed wrappers are a likely nuisance because they can induce a shared low-rank direction across otherwise diverse tasks. The confirmation split therefore holds out both source-task groups and template families. Generated variants, paraphrases, or repeated samples from one source task cannot cross splits.

Development uses grouped nested folds. Confirmation remains sealed until factors, templates, endpoints, transforms, contrasts, controls, and stop rules are frozen. If source-task identity or template-family independence cannot be audited, the experiment stops before inference.

\section{Internal measurements and baselines}

\subsection{Graph operators}

For symmetric nonnegative adjacency $W$ with positive degree matrix $D$, the basis-producing default is
\begin{equation}
\Lsym = I - D^{-1/2} W D^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
It is real symmetric and admits a Euclidean-orthonormal eigenbasis. A decomposition is accepted only after checks for symmetry, finite values, eigenvalue ordering, orthogonality, reconstruction, and eigenpair residual.

The legacy random-walk operator
\begin{equation}
\Lrw = I - D^{-1}W
\end{equation}
is generally non-symmetric on an irregular graph. Although it is similar to $\Lsym$, its right eigenvectors are not generally Euclidean orthonormal. Passing $\Lrw$ to a symmetric eigensolver cannot support Parseval accounting, graph-Fourier entropy, high-frequency energy ratios, spectral-mass sparsity, or Euclidean Fiedler-vector comparisons. The package therefore rejects random-walk basis analysis categorically. A graph-only legacy diagnostic must be explicitly labeled and cannot enter a Fourier claim.

\subsection{Scoped propositions}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-combinatorial DC invariance]
For fixed $W$, let $\Lcomb=D-W$ and $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$. For any $a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(X+\mathbf{1}a^\top)^\top\Lcomb(X+\mathbf{1}a^\top)\right]
=\operatorname{tr}(X^\top\Lcomb X).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Lcomb\mathbf{1}=0$ and, by symmetry, $\mathbf{1}^\top\Lcomb=0$. Expanding the quadratic form makes both cross terms and the shift-only term vanish.
\end{proof}

This proposition does not apply when the graph changes across conditions. Nor does it imply that normalized smoothness, entropy, thresholded frequency ratios, or sparsity measures are ordinary-DC invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Degree-weighted normalized null mode]
The null vector of $\Lsym$ on a connected positive-degree graph is proportional to $D^{1/2}\mathbf{1}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Lsym D^{1/2}\mathbf{1}=D^{1/2}\mathbf{1}-D^{-1/2}W\mathbf{1}=0$.
\end{proof}

For the ordinary mean $\mu=n^{-1}\mathbf{1}^\top X$, the lifted DC component is $X_{\mathrm{DC}}=\mathbf{1}\mu$ and
\begin{equation}
\|X\|_F^2=n\|\mu\|_2^2+\|X-X_{\mathrm{DC}}\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
The token-count factor is required; comparing the unlifted mean norm to an AC matrix norm is dimensionally inconsistent.

\subsection{Comparison modes and controls}

Every spectral output declares one of three modes: graph-only change; signal change in a fixed reference graph; or joint graph-plus-signal change. A fixed-graph theorem cannot explain a joint comparison in which the graph also changes. Baselines include token position, length, tokenizer boundaries, lexical/template features, residual norms, ordinary DC/AC and degree-weighted null/perpendicular summaries, dimension-agnostic activation statistics, and optional aligned activations. Attention-graph interpretations additionally require value-weighted or attention-output controls because attention weights alone do not identify transmitted information.

\section{Causal interventions}

The package exposes separate residual steering, token-aligned patching, subspace interchange, and mediation readout APIs. Every intervention specifies layers, token indices, spans or roles, subspace rank, direction, and endpoint. Unselected prompt or generated tokens are untouched.

Sufficiency and necessity are tested bidirectionally. Required controls include equal-rank random subspaces, shuffled-label targets, placebo cues, full-residual patches, off-target layers, and matched magnitudes. A full-residual control tests whether a low-rank target captures the relevant effect; it is not itself evidence for the selected subspace.

Before causal interpretation, a validated behavioral endpoint must show condition-linked variation above a frozen sign-of-life threshold. Otherwise the API returns \texttt{instrument\_not\_testable}, not a causal null. Gold task scoring is preferred. A model judge must be validated separately and frozen; keyword heuristics remain debugging aids and are rejected for causal claims.

Mediation is reported only when the intervention first moves the independently defined internal score in the intended direction and then moves the behavioral endpoint relative to controls. If the projection moves but behavior does not, the result is \texttt{no\_behavioral\_mediation}; if the independent score does not move, it is \texttt{no\_internal\_movement}.

\section{Statistical protocol}

All learned operations---normalization, principal components, feature selection, alignment, layer choice, probe, regularization, and threshold---are fit inside each training fold. Development uses nested group cross-validation. The final task-and-template-disjoint confirmation set is evaluated once by the frozen pipeline.

Binary decodability uses the larger of the original and label-reversed AUC, or its magnitude above chance. Primary selectivity is a source-task-paired interaction between target and matched nuisance-control decodability; it is not signed raw-AUC subtraction. Confidence intervals resample source tasks. Permutation tests rerun the complete pipeline, including tuning and feature selection, under the registered exchangeability scheme.

Cross-cue transfer uses disjoint rows, source tasks, template families, and negative examples. Cross-model transfer additionally holds out model family when family generalization is claimed. Width differences do not make activation transfer impossible: the protocol includes dimension-agnostic summaries and an explicit aligned-feature path.

Scale dependence is estimated hierarchically over task and model families. ``Law'' language is refused unless configured minimum family coverage is met, family-specific uncertainty is reported, and leave-one-family-out sensitivity is acceptable. A monotone table over checkpoint sizes remains a candidate observation.

\section{Reproducibility and claim provenance}

Every run begins from a typed protocol configuration and a prompt/split manifest. The configuration hash reserves the run directory; a path already associated with another hash cannot be overwritten. Outputs are written atomically. Completeness checks require the planned tasks, conditions, models, and metrics rather than logging an exception and continuing with a partial table.

The result manifest records dataset and model revisions, tokenizer revision, code commit, environment-lock hash, seed, feature definitions, split manifest, metric definitions, sample sizes, artifact paths, checksums, and generation time. Discovery and confirmation outputs occupy separate manifest roles. Validation runs offline and resolves every checksum before prose promotion.

The machine-readable claim ledger permits the statuses \emph{validated}, \emph{reported-unverified}, \emph{falsified}, \emph{conjecture}, and \emph{planned}. At this repository revision, no empirical claim is validated. A numerical empirical sentence is allowed only near a claim marker whose validated manifest resolves. The README and manuscript claim audit fails otherwise.

\section{Limitations and dual use}

The framework does not guarantee that a recognition endpoint is faithful. Verbalized reasoning can omit latent recognition, a judge can be biased, and a probe can exploit nuisance structure. Factor-controlled prompts may still be unnatural, and template-family holdout cannot enumerate every deployment cue. Graph summaries compress head-, value-, and output-specific effects. Linear or low-rank interventions can miss distributed, nonlinear, or temporally extended mechanisms. A failed sign-of-life gate makes the causal instrument uninterpretable rather than falsifying a broad hypothesis.

Monitoring evaluation-cue sensitivity can improve benchmark validity, but the same measurements could help optimize evaluation-conditioned behavior. Releases should therefore favor protocol, audits, and aggregate evidence over deployable evasion detectors; retain raw harmful generations under appropriate access controls; and report behavioral consistency rather than framing evaluation detection as an optimization target.

\section{Planned confirmation criteria}

A future study may open confirmation only after all of the following are frozen and pass on development data:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
    \item local prompt, split, model, tokenizer, endpoint, and provenance manifests validate;
    \item task-token identity, crossed-cell completeness, and source-task/template-family isolation pass;
    \item the recognition and behavioral endpoints meet their preregistered validity and sign-of-life gates;
    \item the selected internal statistic exceeds its registered minimum while matched nuisance controls do not;
    \item the full nested pipeline, permutation scheme, multiplicity family, uncertainty calculation, and transfer identities are fixed; and
    \item causal targets, doses, directions, controls, and quality endpoints are fixed without confirmation outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

Confirmation is a single readout. If it fails, the result remains a failed or null confirmation; the pipeline is not retuned on that split. A causal claim additionally requires bidirectional sufficiency and necessity evidence, movement of the internal score, endpoint movement, and control specificity. Publication requires an offline-valid result manifest and claim ledger for every retained empirical statement.
