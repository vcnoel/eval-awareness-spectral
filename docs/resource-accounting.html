Surviving artifacts cannot identify the grant's RU rate

Question

Can the grant's actual RU-to-H100-hour rate and experiment-level burn be reconstructed from the surviving scientific artifacts?

  • Known balance. 250 RU allocated, 20.6 RU remaining, and 229.4 RU consumed.
  • Known accelerator time. One preserved ledger records 14,427 H100-seconds, or 4.0075 H100-hours.
  • Unknown charges. No surviving record maps RU charges to that ledger or to eight other scientific experiment roots.

Evidence

  • Rate is unidentified. Missing GPU and non-GPU charges prevent a central or lower RU/H100-hour estimate.
  • Ceiling is 57.2427. Assigning every consumed RU to the only documented H100 time gives an over-attribution ceiling, not a billing rate.
  • Balance cannot plan safely. At that ceiling, 20.6 RU equal 21.59 H100-minutes, but unmeasured consumption invalidates that conversion for approvals.

What is missing

  • per-job GPU type and count.
  • billed versus wall-clock seconds.
  • failed, cancelled, and image-import charges.
  • CPU, judge, storage, and orchestration RU categories.
  • experiment-level RU aggregation.
  • durable opening and closing RU balances.

A six-hour wall-limit note cannot be converted to H100-hours because accelerator type and count are missing.

Recommendation

  • mandatory durable resource_usage.json per experiment.
  • per-job ledger keyed by job ID and experiment EID.
  • live RU balance and cap controls.
  • planned-versus-actual reporting.
  • billing exports available in the Lab.

Answer

  • Reconstruction fails. The surviving artifacts do not identify the actual RU/H100-hour rate or experiment-level burn.
  • The ceiling is limited. 57.2427 RU/H100-hour is useful only as an explicit over-attribution ceiling.
  • Product telemetry is required. Durable per-job billing and balance records are necessary before a fixed-grant researcher can safely approve compute.
